# Integrated market judgement

## Decision discipline

Start with the parent wave count, not with a news headline. Then ask whether price internals and leaders confirm it. Macro and policy determine whether a technical correction is more likely to remain a correction or broaden into a regime change.

The conclusion is state-dependent. A C-wave endpoint, a wave-5 objective, or a reversal target appears only when that structure is the live main branch. When evidence is mixed, choose the most likely path rather than withholding a forecast; use price and evidence triggers to define when that forecast must be replaced.

| Layer | Bullish confirmation | Bearish confirmation | What it cannot do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parent wave | valid wave 4 completes and price breaks wave-3 high | wave-4 non-overlap is violated | predict a policy surprise |
| Internals | breadth expands and turnover supports the advance | breadth contracts and turnover expands on the decline | override a hard wave invalidation |
| Leaders | anchors and narrative leaders jointly break upward | leaders broadly lose weekly trend support | turn one leader into the whole market |
| Liquidity/macro | credit and money conditions improve without deterioration in activity | credit/money momentum weakens alongside activity stress | set a precise index target |
| Policy/events | verified, implementable easing or earnings/reform surprise | verified tightening, supply shock, or risk event | replace price confirmation |

## Report pattern

State one main forecast and no more than two alternatives. For each, include the decisive price level, time window, leader/breadth behaviour, and the event or macro variable that can force a re-judgement. Lead with the main forecast, not a confidence label.
