# Correction patterns

Corrections are not all identical `A-B-C` lines. Identify the simplest compliant pattern first and preserve an alternative when confirmation is missing.

| Pattern | Internal form | Typical behaviour | Practical test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zigzag | 5-3-5 | sharp, directional correction | A and C are usually forceful; C often equals A or extends 1.618×A |
| Flat | 3-3-5 | broad sideways correction | B retraces most of A and may exceed A's start; C then breaks away |
| Triangle | 3-3-3-3-3 | converging range, commonly wave 4 or B | label A-B-C-D-E only after alternating converging pivots exist |
| Combination | W-X-Y (or W-X-Y-X-Z) | prolonged, complex sideways correction | do not force a single ABC after the first pattern has completed |

## Reporting rule

Write the evidence before the name: pivot sequence, retracement ratio, duration, and confirmation/invalidity. If only an initial high-low-high sequence exists, report `A-B-C down candidate`; it does not become an established zigzag until C has confirmed. A flat requires a sufficiently deep B retracement; a triangle requires all five converging legs.

## Five-leg clarification

Do not describe a correction as simply “three waves” or “five waves” without naming the degree:

- A parent zigzag or flat has three labelled legs, while its C leg normally contains five lower-degree waves.
- A triangle has five labelled corrective legs, each normally corrective internally.
- A combination may show more than five visible swings without becoming a motive wave.

When calling a rebound `C4` and the next decline `C5`, list or mark `待证` for C1-C3 first. A second test of the low is compatible with C5 but is not required: C5 may extend, form a near-double-bottom, or truncate.
